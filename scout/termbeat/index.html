The vision

TERMBEAT started as one idea: what if the terminal itself became an instrument. Scale that up and it gets big fast, a room built like a film set, walls of screens, a projector throwing code across smoke, a rack of phones each running a different theme side by side. Somewhere in the middle of it, someone is playing music instead of a keyboard. Every command that types itself, every block that drops, every line that pings back, all of it driven by a snare hit or a bassline instead of a person pressing enter. The performer is not a programmer. They are a conductor, and the orchestra is a wall of terminals losing its mind in time with the track.

How it works

The app listens to the audio itself rather than counting on its own clock. A Web Audio analyser splits the signal into two bands, low frequencies for kicks and high frequencies for hi-hats, each tracking its own rolling average. When either band spikes far enough above its recent average, that counts as a beat. Every beat triggers something, a burst of characters typed into a fake command line, a block dropping in the Visualizer, another line of a build project appearing. Quiet stretches get their own behavior too, a slow one character at a time trickle, like someone thinking between phrases instead of a hard stop. A sensitivity slider in the topbar tunes how far above its own rolling average a band has to spike before it counts as a beat, lower for a track that hits constantly, higher for one that only pops on the big moments.

The visualizer

Seven self playing modes live behind the Visualizer tab, and none of them take input. Bars is a straightforward ASCII equalizer reading the live frequency spectrum off the same analyser the beat detection uses. Waterfall takes that same spectrum and scrolls it downward frame by frame instead of redrawing in place, so the last several seconds of the track's shape stack up on screen like a rolling printout. Matrix drops columns of code rain at their own independent speeds, each one surging faster for a moment on a beat. Snow drifts flakes slowly downward with a gentle sideways sway, gusting sideways on a beat instead of speeding up. Tetris drops a real piece every beat, locks it when it lands, clears full rows, and wipes the board clean if it ever fills up, unattended, forever. Snake path finds toward food on its own logic every beat and resets itself the moment it traps itself. Pong is a ball bouncing around the grid with no paddles, picking up speed on strong hits. None of them are games to play. They are games watching the music instead.

Easter eggs

Nine terminal styles, Paper, Hacker, Kali, Android, Linux, Windows, PowerShell, MS-DOS, and Mac / Lisa, each pull from commands native to that world instead of generic text reused everywhere. Every joke command now prints its own matching reply instead of a random one. The Unix side carries the fork bomb, sudo make me a sandwich, man woman, make love, the sl train, fortune piped through cowsay, the eject tray prank, a rotating sysadmin excuse of the day, and a telnet call to the old ASCII Star Wars broadcast. Windows and PowerShell get the shutdown prank and its cancel, the jdbgmgr.exe teddy bear hoax, and a sandwich cmdlet that does not exist. MS-DOS brings ANSI bombs, the Good Times hoax, a FORMAT C: that always answers N, a 2400 baud BBS dial in, DEBUG listings, and dysentery. Mac / Lisa runs the MacsBug debugger and the Lisa Workshop, with the Stolen from Apple Computer string, WHAT ARE YOU STARING AT?, Clarus the Dogcow saying Moof!, a Sad Mac, and a startup chord played through the speaker. Hacker and Kali add a randomized framework banner, a PWNED banner, and a movie style takeover of a documentation only address where no packets ever leave the page. On the Android theme, termux-toast, termux-vibrate, and termux-tts-speak trigger a real on screen toast, a vibration that matches the printed duration, and text to speech through the browser. Every theme can open a themed document in its native editor. The Build button rotates through four small real projects, Tetris, Pong, Snake, and a todo list. Your terminal style, visualizer mode, and sensitivity are remembered on this device.
