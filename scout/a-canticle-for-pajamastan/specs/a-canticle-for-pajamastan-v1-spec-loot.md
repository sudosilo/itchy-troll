A Canticle for Pajamastan, loot spec, version one.

This document describes how drops are rolled when an enemy dies or a container is broken. It is restated in our own terms from observed behavior of a classic console action RPG. Every chance, weight and range lives in the loot tuning file, and the values there are ours.

The random number source is split into separate channels, one per kind of roll: the drop gate, category, rare roll, tier, bonus type, bonus amount, special roll, currency and so on. Each channel is its own seeded stream. Adding a new roll to one part of the system never shifts the results of another part, so a seed always replays the same loot.

An enemy drop runs in this order. First the rare roll. Every enemy type can have a rare entry naming a specific item and a tiny probability. If the rare roll succeeds, that item drops and nothing else is rolled. Second the drop gate. Every enemy type has a drop chance out of one hundred, and if the gate roll misses, nothing drops. Third the category. The roll picks between currency, a consumable tool, or the enemy type's signature category, which is one of weapon, armor, shield or charm. Enemies that come out of a special variant skip currency and only roll between tool and signature. Fourth the item itself is built for that category.

A weapon is built by choosing a weapon family from weights set for the current area, then a tier from a weighted table offset by the area's depth, so deeper areas push tiers upward. The tier is lowered by a fixed amount and floored at zero, which gives shallow areas a good share of starter gear. A weapon then rolls a refinement value from its own weighted table. Then it rolls up to three bonus slots. Each slot picks a target family from area weights and an amount from a weighted table, and amounts snap to steps of five. Slots are cleaned after rolling: a slot that repeats a family already taken is emptied, and once a slot is empty every slot after it is emptied too, so bonuses always fill from the front with no repeats. Finally, a non rare weapon rolls the area's special chance. On success it gets a special effect whose strength is drawn from a tier scaled by a random ratio.

Armor and shields are built by choosing a base piece by tier, then rolling a defense bonus and an evasion bonus. Each bonus is the piece's maximum bonus multiplied by a random ratio between zero and one. A charm is chosen from a star rated pool, where the star ceiling for the area is multiplied by a random ratio and the charm is drawn from the bucket at or below that star count. A tool is drawn from a weighted table for the area, and spells drawn as tools also roll a spell level from a level range for the area. Currency is a random amount within the area's currency range.

Containers use a different path. They roll the rare table first, filtered to rare entries that name containers in this area. Then they pick a category from their own weight table, which leans heavily toward tools and currency, and build the item the same way enemies do.

After any item is built, restriction rules are applied, such as stripping specials in challenge modes or replacing items a mode doesn't allow, and items flagged as rare get a rare marker so the game can play the rare drop effect.
