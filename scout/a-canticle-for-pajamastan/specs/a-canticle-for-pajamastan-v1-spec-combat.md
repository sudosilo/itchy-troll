A Canticle for Pajamastan, combat spec, version one.

This document describes how a single attack is resolved between an attacker and a defender. It is written from observed behavior of a classic console action RPG and restated in our own terms. Every coefficient named here lives in the combat tuning file, and the values in that file are ours.

Every fighter carries a stat block with seven values: power, mind, evasion, vitality, defense, accuracy and luck. Power drives physical damage. Mind drives spell damage and spell points. Evasion makes the fighter harder to hit. Vitality feeds maximum health. Defense subtracts from incoming damage. Accuracy makes the fighter's own attacks land. Luck drives critical hits.

An attack is always one of a small set of attack kinds. Each kind carries an accuracy multiplier and a damage multiplier. The quick kind hits often and softly, the heavy kind hits hard and misses more, and the special kind sits between them and also triggers whatever special effect the weapon carries. Attacks chain into combos. Each later step of a combo receives a flat accuracy bonus on top of the kind multiplier, so a player who lands the opening swing is rewarded with steadier follow ups. A combo resets when the chain is broken by a pause longer than the combo window.

Hit chance is resolved first. A fraction of the attacker's accuracy is multiplied by the attack kind's accuracy multiplier, then a flat base chance and the combo step bonus are added. A fraction of the defender's evasion is subtracted. For ranged attacks, a distance penalty is subtracted as well, growing in a straight line with the flat ground distance between attacker and target, so long shots miss more often. Melee attacks skip the distance penalty. The result is read as a percentage. A uniform random roll between zero and one hundred is drawn, and the attack lands when the roll is at or below the chance. A chance at or below zero always misses. A miss produces a miss popup and no damage.

Damage is resolved only for hits. The base is a fraction of the attacker's power plus a random portion of the weapon's power spread, so every weapon has a low end and a high end and each hit falls somewhere between them. On top of the base sits an elemental bonus. Weapons carry up to three bonus slots, each naming a target family and a percentage. When the defender belongs to that family, the elemental part of the hit is raised by that percentage. A fraction of the defender's defense is subtracted, along with any armor value on the specific body part that was struck, which lets large enemies have armored and soft spots. A result below zero becomes zero and is shown as a blocked hit. A positive result is multiplied by the attack kind's damage multiplier.

Critical hits are resolved after damage. The attacker's luck is capped at a ceiling. A random roll is drawn between zero and a crit divisor, and the hit is critical when the roll is at or below the capped luck. Players and enemies use different crit divisors, so enemies can be made more or less crit prone than players. A critical hit multiplies the damage by the crit multiplier and plays the critical effect instead of the normal hit effect.

A single swing can strike several body parts of one defender, and each part is resolved on its own: its own defense subtraction, its own crit roll. The parts' damage is summed and subtracted from the defender's health in one step. A defender at or below zero health is flagged as dying. Damage taken also charges the defender's super meter by a fixed factor of the total, so getting beaten on builds toward a comeback move.

A fighter can be flagged invulnerable, which still runs the hit and effects but skips the health loss. That's used for dodge rolls, cutscenes and freshly revived players.
