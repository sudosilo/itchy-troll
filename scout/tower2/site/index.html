How to play

Build a tower that reaches the green target line, then survive three tests in a row: its own weight, a steady wind, and an earthquake.

Building

Tap a support (the dark triangles) or any joint to pick it, then tap a grid point to place a beam. The new end stays picked so you can keep going. Tap the picked joint again to let go. Beams can be up to 4.5 m long and cables up to 30 m.

Why towers fall

Every joint is a pin, so it can rotate freely. A square of beams folds flat. A triangle cannot change shape, so diagonal braces are what keep a tower standing.

Materials

Wood is cheap and light but weak.

Steel is strong when pulled and when squeezed, but long steel beams buckle.

Concrete takes huge squeezing loads, is weaker when pulled, and is heavy, which hurts in an earthquake.

Cable is light, cheap, and can reach far, but it only pulls. Push on it and it goes slack.

Road is what the truck drives on in bridge levels.

Bridges

From level 13 on, you build bridges. Lay road from one cliff edge to the other, then hold it up with trusses, arches, piers, or cables. The bridge settles under its own weight, then a truck drives across. If the road breaks or sags too far, the truck falls.

Reading the test

During a test, beams shift toward red as they near their limit. A beam that goes past it breaks. Some broken beams are survivable, but if the tower drops below the target line, the test fails.

Moving around

Drag with one finger to pan. Pinch or use the buttons to zoom. Your build for each level is kept on this device.
