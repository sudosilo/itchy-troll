Decimal Day Clock

Block
0.00.00

One day is 86,400 SI seconds. Each tick is one SI second, the same length as today. A minute is 100 ticks. A block is 100 minutes, which is 10,000 ticks.

The day starts at your local midnight. Blocks 0 through 7 fill 80,000 ticks. The last 6,400 ticks of the day are the short hour, counted as 64 minutes of 100 ticks.

The short hour is the special hour. By default it starts at 10:13:20 PM on the old clock, at the end of the day where people are usually done, so anything done inside it is on purpose.

Sexagesimal civil time
0:00:00 AM

The normal clock mixes bases. Seconds and minutes count to 60, hours count to 24, and days count to 7. Every addition needs a carry in a different base. 2h 45m plus 1h 30m only works if you stop and convert.

The units were set by habit from Babylonian base 60 and the Egyptian 24 hour split, long before anyone could measure time. The second was fitted to them later.

It also lies about the sun. Time zones and daylight saving move the clock an hour while the sun stays put, so the same moment has a different number depending on where you stand and what month it is.

The decimal day has one base. Every unit is 100 of the one below it, so math is moving a decimal point. 3.50.00 plus 1.25.00 is 4.75.00. Durations add, subtract and scale in your head. Fast math makes planning, time management and setting expectations easier, and less friction in the day is better for the soul.

Since 1970
0.00000

Computers count time as a running total of ticks since midnight UTC on January 1, 1970. That total is the calendar computers use. No hours, no zones, no daylight saving.

The top number is whole days since then, with the part of the day as a decimal. The line under it is the same moment as a plain tick count.

Day count
0.00000

This is how much of the current day has passed. It starts at 0 at your local midnight and climbs toward 1. When it reaches the whole number 1, the day is over and the count resets to 0 for the next day.

A milliday is one thousandth of a day, 86.4 SI seconds. A centiday is one hundredth of a day. So 0.392 of a day is 392 millidays or 39.2 centidays. The whole number of millidays is the same amount as the decimal day count, just counted in thousandths.

Day notepad

Days are counted up from January 1, 1970, using your local date. An orbit is one trip around the sun, 365 or 366 days. A decaday is 10 days, a month is 30 days, and a hectoday is 100 days. Each orbit is 12 months, 360 days, then a short decaday of 5 or 6 days that holds the leftover, so every orbit starts over at day 0 with whole numbers.

Each page is one hectoday: three full ones, then a short hectoday of 65 or 66 days. Each row is a decaday. A darker line starts each month, which is every 3 rows. Tinted cells are the short hectoday, and the darker tint is the short decaday. The orange bar marks day 0 of a new orbit. There are no 7 day weeks.

The small number under each day is the old calendar month and day, for correlation. Tap a day to write a note. A dot marks days with a note. Notes are saved in this browser on this device only.
