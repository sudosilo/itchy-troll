How it works
Every block is built to one standard size, so every cubby slot and every door panel is identical and any panel fits any opening on any block. The tech lives on the door: speakers, pico projectors, LED wash, strobes, lasers, smoke and sensors all mount to the panel and work with the door shut. Swapping a function means swapping the door. Each door hangs on spring loaded hinge pins on the inside, like a toolbox latch, so squeezing the pins frees the panel, and an electric solenoid holds it shut with a click. A stuck door can be reached from the inside by opening the cubby from another face. Blocks join with motorized twist lock pins. Every corner and every shelf line on the frame carries a casting that is both a pin and a receiver, like the genderless docking rings on spacecraft, so any face of any block can lock to any face of another, standing, lying down or stacked. When castings line up, the pins drive into the sloped receivers, twist, and draw the blocks tight, and each pin reports back when it is locked. A locked block cannot be moved until its pins are released. Inside, folded steel shelves with a hollow center stack into a full height cable chase, four corner rods lock every shelf, a power strip runs the full height, and a sealed lead acid UPS is bolted into the bottom cubby so the block can tip, lie down or stack. Blocks stack automatically when moved on top of each other, and a deck laid across two towers bridges them. Weights and power are rough planning estimates.
