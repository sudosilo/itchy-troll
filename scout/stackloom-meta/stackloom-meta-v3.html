How Full Meta Packet works

You run a game studio that pumps out knockoffs of knockoffs. Every game you sell is the same engine with a new skin slapped on it, built for whoever is paying.

Your security team hacks rival studios that are doing the exact same thing. Your lawyers sue those same rivals for making knockoffs.

That is the whole point. Keep the cash coming in long enough to see how far the hypocrisy goes.

Orders and terminals

Customers show up on the Orders tab wanting a themed game. Accept one, put it on a terminal, and the terminal builds it. When it is done you can play test it, then ship it and get paid. Late or buggy games pay less and cost reputation, and reputation brings in more customers.

Staff

Terminals are the machines that build games. Agents are AI programs, each one running up to three terminals by itself, accepting orders, building, and shipping, but they never play test. Devs are people who supervise up to three agents each, so builds go faster with fewer bugs. Everybody with a paycheck has a performance meter that slides unless the lobby has coffee, slackers work slower, and you can fire anyone from the Team tab. Game testers play every finished build, fix most bugs, and ship it for you, and managers make their testers faster and sharper. Everybody gets paid every night whether they did anything or not.

Product lines

Senior hires open new product lines, each one a new engine with a pile of skins: sudoku, bubble shooters, card games, lane runners, match three, pull out puzzles, energy and jobs games, crop timers, and vertical shooters. The Games tab keeps a sample of each so you can play what you are selling.

The tower

The Tower tab is your building from the side. Tap an empty slot to build a room: terminal bays, server closets for agents, dev pods, QA rooms, hacker dens, legal offices, marketing and sales, design lofts for senior hires, and perks like the coffee bar and the cafeteria. Rooms decide how many people you can hire. Bigger offices add floors. The same tab shows the competition: every rival's tower on the skyline, the market share pie, and who ships what.

Hiring and HR

Every hire comes from a stack of three resumes, each with a one line pitch and bars for skill, focus, and loyalty. Better people ask for more. Build an HR office and hire a recruiter to scout better people, set a minimum skill, pay signing bonuses, run job ads through marketing, and run background checks through legal. Skip the checks and sooner or later somebody turns out to have stolen their last studio's code, and the lawsuit lands on you.

Reverse engineering

Rival studios sometimes ship a real original game. The reverse engineering lab at the bottom of the Tower tab tears one apart overnight. Slide the effort up, and when the bar fills your own copy goes on the terminals under a new name, sells like crazy for a few nights, and takes a bite out of the rival. Push harder and it goes faster, costs more, and the rival is more likely to sue. Lawyers help.

The office

Bigger offices hold more terminals and more staff, and cost more rent. Upgrades on the Office tab make the whole studio faster or cleaner.

Past the campus the buildings get stupid. The skyscraper, the megatower, and finally the billion dollar arcology with more floors than anybody needs. Up there you can build coffee booths on every other floor, bedroom pods so nobody goes home, a medic pod, a gym, a grand lobby, and private floors where your best people each get a whole floor to themselves. Or skip all of it, fire the sales team, and live off royalties. Your call.

Carrot or stick

Once the studio moves into the shared studio, pick a management style on the Team tab. Stick pays below market and runs on fear: performance slides faster, firing someone scares everyone else into working, and people at rock bottom quit. Carrot pays what people are worth: payroll climbs, but so does performance. Raises bump someone's pay and performance right away, with diminishing returns, because past a point no amount of money keeps a grown man child focused on his day job.

Perks and house rules

The Office tab sells perks that lift everyone's performance every night: cheap soda and snack machines for a stick shop, a coffee bar, a lounge, and daily catering that costs more as the team grows. Hire a manager and you can set house rules: fire anyone who sinks below a line, and give a raise to anyone who stays above a line for enough nights in a row. The lines show up as red and green ticks on every performance meter.

In house games

Every dev on the Team tab has a Working on menu. Leave them on customer orders and they supervise agents. Point one at an engine and they build Full Meta Packet Originals on it all day, no terminal and no customer, straight onto whatever store shelves you have deals with. Put every dev on puzzle games, stop taking orders, and live off the royalties if the backlist can carry you.

Variety, mashups, and stores

Royalties never stop: every game you ever ship stays on store shelves earning a little forever, so a few hundred forgotten games pay somebody's wages, and an old flop can turn into a sleeper hit overnight. Every order comes with a real product title, like Turbo Burrito Jam Remastered or Lil Fang Feud Idle. It also comes in a mobile, desktop, or console version, and some get a finish like foil, holographic, rainbow, glow in the dark, glitch, or retro CRT that shows up on screen when you play it. Lite versions are free to play: they pay half up front and milk microtransactions later. Remasters only show up for games you already shipped, and they build fast. Mashups splice two skins on the same engine, pay more, and break more. The mashup machine on the Games tab builds mashups on spec under your own label, and storefront deals on the Office tab put every game you have shipped on the shelf to earn a little every night.

Marketing and sales

Marketers run ad campaigns that pump one product line for a few days, so you can ride a streamer craze harder or dig out of a rival's release. Salespeople get more money out of every order, keep customers waiting longer, and turn happy customers into repeat clients. Coffee is for closers, so the lobby coffee bar makes sales squeeze harder.

Game Smash parties

Every so often the office throws a hackathon. For a day everybody builds much faster on pizza and energy drinks and customers show up for the buzz, but the catering bill hits up front and tired people write more bugs. A rumor warns you a day or two ahead.

The market

The industry moves under you. A new graphics card launches, a rival floods the market, a streamer makes one kind of game blow up, the app store raises its cut, the cloud goes down. Each one changes what customers order and what they pay. A rumor shows up on the Orders tab before the big ones hit, so plan for them.

Vulnerabilities

Now and then a CVE hits one of your engines and every game you shipped on it is exposed. That line freezes, free patch jobs pile up, unpatched games cost money every night, and anything still unpatched at the deadline gets fined and pulled from stores. Security engineers patch without using a terminal and make the whole mess cheaper.

Mischief

When your security engineers have nothing to patch, they dig up exploits in rival games. Leak one and the rival's customers come to you. Sell one to a broker for cash. Or report it like a good citizen for reputation. Leaking and selling build heat, and heat is your chance of getting caught.

Lawyers

Lawyers shrink every fine, soften the hit when you get caught, and cool off heat faster. When nothing is on fire, they send cease and desist letters to rivals over their lookalike games, and sometimes a settlement check comes back.

How it ends

Whenever you've had enough, go to the Office tab and either retire or sell the studio. Retiring scores your cash plus a year of royalties, which rewards a big backlist. Selling scores what a buyer pays for the whole operation, which rewards a big tower and a big staff. Then put your three letter tag on the shared high score board.

How you lose

End a day below zero and the top bar turns red. End too many days in a row below zero and the studio folds. Your run score counts revenue, games shipped, and reputation, minus the buggy games you pushed out the door, and your best runs are saved on the Office tab.
